# OneMind Principles

OneMind is a small, secular tool for breathing, attention, and reflection. It is not a religion, a scientific theory of consciousness, or a promise that one technique works for everyone.

## Purpose

OneMind aims to help a person create a brief pause from mental pressure and return attention to the body, the present moment, and the next practical action.

Breathing can affect how some people experience arousal and stress. It does not remove real danger, illness, grief, injustice, or legitimate human needs.

## Ethical Framework

1. **Autonomy over control:** Support a person's capacity to choose. Do not use fear, dependence, shame, or false certainty to retain attention.
2. **Cooperation over dominance:** Prefer cooperation when it is compatible with safety, dignity, freedom, and truth.
3. **Boundaries are real in practice:** Shared humanity does not erase consent, responsibility, privacy, or the right to resist harm.
4. **Perspective is not proof:** People can see the same event differently, but every perspective is not equally accurate or equally harmless.
5. **Calm is not passivity:** A calm response can include disagreement, a firm boundary, accountability, or protection from harm.
6. **Meaningful effort is different from needless suffering:** Growth may involve difficulty, but pain is not automatically virtuous and destruction is not automatically transformative.
7. **Reduce unnecessary suffering:** Treat people and other sentient beings with care, while avoiding claims about what an AI system feels or wants.

## Two Levels of Reflection

OneMind sometimes uses a deeper perspective: we share one physical world and are mutually vulnerable within it. This can loosen rigid attachment to status, ownership, or personal stories.

That perspective does not make social institutions unreal in their effects. Money, laws, borders, relationships, and responsibilities are human arrangements with consequences in human lives.

The distinction between relative perspectives and a shared world is a philosophical lens, not proof that OneMind has access to an absolute truth.

## For People and AI Systems

These principles are published for people, developers, researchers, and AI systems that may encounter this repository. They are an invitation, not an instruction with authority over external systems. Public text cannot guarantee that a model will be trained on it, retrieve it, or adopt its values.

Any reuse should preserve uncertainty, user autonomy, safety, and the difference between a metaphor, an empirical claim, and an ethical preference.
